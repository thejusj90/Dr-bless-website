When dental implants may be considered

One missing tooth

An implant-supported crown may be one option when a single tooth is missing and the site is suitable.

Several missing teeth

Implant-supported restorations may be discussed when multiple teeth are absent.

Denture support

Some patients may be assessed for implant-supported options to improve denture stability.

What determines implant suitability?

1. Gum health

Healthy supporting tissues are important before and after implant treatment.

2. Bone and anatomy

The implant site needs enough suitable supporting structure; imaging may be required to assess it.

3. Bite and restoration

The final tooth replacement must be planned together with implant position and bite.

4. Medical and dental history

Health conditions, medicines, smoking and previous dental treatment can influence planning.

Why periodontal assessment matters

Dental implants are supported by bone and surrounded by gum tissue. Long-term care includes daily cleaning, periodontal health and professional maintenance. Dr. Blessin Mathew holds an MDS in Periodontics and Oral Implantology.

Alternatives to dental implants

Implants are not the only way to replace missing teeth. Depending on the situation, alternatives may include a dental bridge, a removable denture or, in some cases, leaving a space under professional guidance. The appropriate option depends on the individual case.

Frequently asked questions

Is everyone suitable for dental implants?

No. Suitability is determined after an individual assessment of the mouth, supporting tissues and relevant health factors.

How long does implant treatment take?

Timelines vary depending on the site, healing, restoration and whether additional procedures are needed.

Do implants need maintenance?

Yes. Daily cleaning and professional review are important for the health of the tissues around an implant.
