Loose teeth in adults

A loose adult tooth needs professional evaluation

Permanent teeth are not normally mobile. Looseness can be associated with gum and bone support problems, injury, bite forces or other conditions.

Can a loose tooth be saved?Sometimes. The answer depends on why the tooth is mobile, how much support remains and whether the underlying cause can be treated.

Possible reasons for tooth mobility

Periodontal disease

Loss of gum and bone support around a tooth can lead to increasing mobility.

Trauma

A blow or injury can damage the tooth and its supporting tissues.

Bite-related forces

Excessive or unfavourable forces can contribute to symptoms in some situations.

What a gum-focused assessment may include

  • Checking tooth mobility and gum condition.
  • Periodontal measurements where appropriate.
  • Assessment of plaque, calculus and inflammation.
  • Dental imaging when needed to evaluate supporting bone.
  • Discussion of treatment, stabilisation or replacement options.

Frequently asked questions

Should I avoid chewing on a loose tooth?

Until it is assessed, avoiding heavy pressure on a very mobile or painful tooth is sensible.

Can gum treatment tighten a loose tooth?

Mobility may improve in some cases when inflammation is controlled, but the outcome depends on the amount of remaining support.

Is tooth mobility always caused by gum disease?

No. Trauma and other dental factors can also cause mobility.
