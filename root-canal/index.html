When to book a dental assessment

Persistent tooth pain

Pain that continues, returns or interferes with eating or sleep is a reason to have the tooth examined.

Deep decay

Extensive decay can involve the inner tissues of a tooth and may require further evaluation.

Cracked or damaged tooth

A fracture or previous dental injury may affect the pulp or remaining tooth structure.

What the consultation may include

  • Discussion of the pain, sensitivity or other symptoms.
  • Examination of the tooth and surrounding gums.
  • Tests to help identify the affected tooth where necessary.
  • Dental imaging when clinically indicated.
  • Discussion of whether the tooth can be restored and the available options.

If root canal treatment is recommended

The aim is to treat the inside of the tooth and retain the natural tooth when that is appropriate. The tooth may also need a suitable final restoration depending on how much tooth structure remains and where the tooth is located.

Frequently asked questions

Does every toothache need a root canal?

No. Pain can come from decay, cracks, gums, bite problems, sensitivity and other causes. Diagnosis comes first.

Can a root-canal-treated tooth need a crown?

Sometimes. The final restoration depends on the tooth, remaining structure and clinical findings.

What if the tooth cannot be retained?

If retaining the tooth is not appropriate, extraction and replacement options may be discussed after examination.
